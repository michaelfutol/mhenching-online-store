# China-Direct Sourcing Wave 1 — 2026-08-20

## Strategy

Start with a deliberately small, curated assortment rather than a broad catalog. Default Mhenching test quantity is **12 pieces per product**, even where supplier MOQ is 10. Nothing becomes public inventory until ordered, received, QA-checked, photographed, priced from actual landed cost, and entered into the Mhenching stock flow.

## Selection doctrine

A Wave 1 candidate must be:

- cheap enough for a 12-piece test;
- small/light enough that freight is unlikely to destroy the economics;
- easy to explain in one sentence;
- visually demonstrable in a 6–15 second vertical video;
- useful or satisfying enough to trigger impulse purchase;
- low-complexity to store and sell in the physical shop;
- low-regulatory-risk and low-return-risk relative to electronics/fashion-heavy categories.

## Important economics note

Alibaba product price is **not landed cost**. Before approving a purchase, request or calculate:

- exact variant/specification;
- 12-piece unit price;
- carton dimensions and gross weight;
- China domestic shipping to consolidator if any;
- China-to-Philippines consolidated freight;
- customs/tax/handling where applicable;
- defect allowance;
- local packaging/label cost.

Only then should the app compute true landed cost, gross contribution, margin and reorder threshold.

## Presentation rule

Every product gets a Mhenching-specific micro-story and short demo concept before launch. Marketplace supplier media is reference-only. Final customer listing uses our own product photos/video after receiving samples.

## Physical-store integration

Wave 1 products are intended to be stocked physically in Mhenching as well as listed online. First receiving flow:

`ORDERED → RECEIVED → QA → ACTUAL LANDED COST → SKU → PHOTO/VIDEO → PHYSICAL STOCK → ONLINE DRAFT → APPROVE → PUBLISH`

## Decision gate

Do not place a bulk reorder because a product has high marketplace sales elsewhere. Mhenching's own evidence controls scale:

- sell-through speed;
- walk-in vs online demand;
- ad cost and conversion;
- gross contribution after delivery/packaging;
- defects/returns;
- customer comments/searches;
- supplier consistency.
